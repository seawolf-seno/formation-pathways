# Formation Pathways

## How Pacifica Christian High School Will Use AI to Guide Every Student Toward Who They Were Created to Be

*Prepared for discussion with the Board*

---

## The Premise

Pacifica's advantage has never been curriculum, facilities, or test scores. It is people. When nearly every adult on campus loves God, loves teens, loves their content, teaches with genuine expertise, and loves the mission, students are formed — not just educated. Grace and truth are woven into ordinary Tuesdays, and over four years, teenagers become the people God uniquely created them to be. We call this Ruakh 2.0, and those of us inside the building see it happen at a rate we have not witnessed anywhere else.

The problem is that this transformation is largely invisible and largely unguided by data. Prospective families hear the claim but have to take it on faith. Current families sense it in their kids but can't quite name it. And the guidance we give students about their futures still runs through the same narrow funnel every other school uses — GPA, test scores, college list — even though we believe something far bigger about each student: that God put them on earth for a specific, positive impact on others, and that our job is to help them find it.

This proposal is about closing that gap. In one sentence:

> **Every school can tell you what your child knows. Pacifica will be the first school that can show you who your child is becoming — and where that person will matter most.**

Its governing principle is simple:

**AI works for the adults, behind the scenes. Students stay present, in person, with people.**

And its central asset is something no other school has: **Formation Pathways** — a living, forward-looking map of who each student is becoming and the widest possible range of routes by which they might deploy that becoming in the world.

## Three Answers to the Same Question

Every school is now answering the question "what do we do about AI?" The answers reveal what each school actually believes about kids.

**The public school answer** is defensive and generic. AI arrives as a cheating problem to be policed and a productivity tool to be rationed. The underlying model doesn't change: standardized content, standardized pacing, standardized outcomes, and a guidance system that sorts students into a single funnel by GPA. Purpose, faith, and calling are off the table by design — the questions our students most need answered are the ones a public school is structurally forbidden to ask.

**The Alpha School answer** is bold but inverted. Alpha puts AI *in front of* students — adaptive software compresses academics into two hours a day, and the human role shrinks to that of a guide. It is a genuinely impressive answer to the question "how fast can a student master content?" But it is silent on the question that matters more: *mastery in service of what?* Efficiency is not formation. A student can finish algebra in half the time and still have no idea who they are, why they're here, or what their life is for. Alpha optimizes velocity; it has no theology of the person.

**The Pacifica answer** inverts Alpha's inversion. We put AI *behind* our adults — the scribes and cartographers of formation — while students spend their days in the presence of people who meet our five hiring requirements. Content mastery is table stakes; the product is transformation. Where the public school asks "how do you rank?" and Alpha asks "how fast can you learn?", Pacifica asks the question that has always mattered: **"Who did God make you to be, and what is the largest positive impact you can have on others?"** — and now builds the environment *and* the technology platform to answer it, student by student, with evidence.

That combination — an environment of mission-aligned adults plus a platform that charts unique transformation paths — is something neither the public system nor the AI-first schools can replicate. The public schools lack the mission; the AI-first schools lack the people.

## What We Would Build: Formation Pathways

At the center of this proposal is a longitudinal Formation Pathway for every student, built from hundreds of small, human observations and synthesized with AI.

A word on the name, because it matters. This is not a record, a file, or a transcript-in-waiting. A record looks backward and can be held against you; it curdles into credentialism the moment someone treats it as a score. A **pathway looks forward**. Its only purpose is to answer, with ever-increasing clarity, where this student is headed and how the adults around them can help them get there. It belongs to the student's future, is held in trust by adults who love them, and is never used to gatekeep, rank, or punish. Grace is built into the architecture: moments of failure appear only as they appear in Scripture — as turning points in a redemption story.

Here is how it works in practice. After class, at practice, after chapel, or at the end of the week, a teacher spends twenty to sixty seconds capturing a moment: a voice memo on the walk to the parking lot, a two-tap entry on a phone. "Marcus stayed after class to help a freshman who was lost on the proof — unprompted, patient, no audience." "Sofia pushed back on my reading of the Psalm today with a genuinely better one. Six months ago she wouldn't have spoken."

AI does the rest. It transcribes, tags each observation against a Pacifica Formation Framework the faculty designs (dimensions rooted in grace and truth: spiritual depth, intellectual courage, character under pressure, love of neighbor, emerging superpowers, and sense of calling), and quietly accumulates them. The capture load is deliberately modest and advisor-anchored: advisors carry the deepest responsibility for their ten to twelve advisees, while subject teachers and coaches contribute when a moment genuinely stands out. Even fifteen to twenty-five observations per student per year — a realistic ask spread across eight adults — compounds to well over a hundred witnessed moments by graduation, a picture no single teacher could hold. AI assembles it, and a human always reviews and authors anything that reaches a family. Finding the exact sustainable capture rhythm is the pilot's first job, and we will not scale past what teachers tell us is light.

**The student is a co-author, not a subject.** A Pathway written entirely *about* a teenager — however lovingly — is a file; a Pathway written *with* them is formation. So students contribute their own voice at the moments that matter: a short recorded reflection after Lost Canyon, after an expedition, and at each semester's end, answering questions like "where did you surprise yourself this year?" Students see their full Pathway, may respond to anything in it, and their own words appear alongside their teachers' — because discovering who God made you to be is something done *with* a student, never *to* one. Parents may contribute too; they witness formation the school never sees.

**No student goes unseen.** The same system that captures moments can notice their absence. A simple radar flags any student who hasn't been meaningfully observed in recent weeks — not to generate data, but to prompt an adult to move toward that kid. Every school loses quiet students in the noise; Pacifica becomes, structurally, a school where it is impossible to be invisible. For a community built on loving teens, this may be the platform's most Christian feature.

These are the hundreds of unique data points — not clicks, grades, or screen-time analytics, but witnessed moments of formation captured by adults who meet the five hiring requirements, in dialogue with the students themselves. The data is only as good as the people, which is exactly why Pacifica can do this and almost no one else can.

## The Data Gap: What Canvas Knows vs. What Pathways Witnesses

Every school already has "student data." It is worth being precise about what that data actually is. Canvas — like every LMS — logs thousands of raw events per student: page views, logins, submission timestamps. But nearly all of it is behavioral exhaust. Distill it to signals a human would act on and a semester of Canvas yields perhaps a few dozen meaningful data points per student — grades, missing assignments, quiz scores — all backward-looking, all about content compliance, and none of them about character, faith, relationships, superpowers, or calling. No one has ever understood who a teenager is becoming from a gradebook.

| | **Canvas (typical LMS)** | **Formation Pathways** |
|---|---|---|
| Meaningful signals per student, per year | ~40–80 (grades, submissions, quiz scores) | **500–900 witnessed moments** |
| Over four years | a transcript | **2,000–3,600 moments → a transformation narrative** |
| Who contributes | software logging | teachers (daily), coaches, advisors, student self-evaluations, parents, mentors, retreat & expedition leaders |
| What it measures | content compliance, on time or late | spiritual depth, intellectual courage, character, love of neighbor, superpowers, calling |
| Direction | backward-looking record | forward-looking compass |
| Authored by | no one | adults who love the student, and the student themselves |

The difference is not volume for its own sake — it is that every Pathways data point is a *witnessed human observation*, contributed daily by the whole community around a student and synthesized by AI into something no LMS dashboard can produce: an evidentiary answer to "who is this kid becoming?" Canvas can stay for homework logistics. It simply was never built to see a soul grow, and we should stop pretending any amount of click data will.

## The Pathways Engine: Escaping the College Funnel

Here is where the Pathway becomes more than a beautiful mirror. Most schools — including most excellent Christian schools — still map every student onto one route: grades, test prep, college list, done. But the world has quietly built dozens of legitimate, demanding, high-upside routes into adulthood, each selecting for different superpowers, and almost no high school systematically matches students to them.

The Pathways Engine does. AI maintains a living catalog of what the world's most interesting pathways actually select for, and each year matches every student's accumulating Pathway against it — surfacing the two or three best-fit routes for advisors and families to explore together. The catalog is deliberately wide:

Selective colleges and honors programs remain on the map, but alongside them sit the **Thiel Fellowship** (which pays exceptional young people to build instead of enroll), **Y Combinator** and its Startup School for teen founders with proof-of-work, **Emergent Ventures** and other talent grants that fund raw agency, gap-year fellowships like **Watson** and **Praxis**, skilled-trade apprenticeships that lead to ownership, the service academies, conservatories and film programs for artists, research tracks like **RSI** and **ISEF** for scientists, open-source contribution and the creator economy for builders, and mission and ministry pathways for students whose superpower is shepherding people.

What these non-traditional assessors have discovered — and what our theology already told us — is that the strongest predictor of impact is not a GPA. It is demonstrated agency: what a student has actually built, served, led, or made when no one required it. A Formation Pathway is precisely a four-year evidentiary base of that. By senior year, every Pacifica student has both a narrative of who they are *and* a portfolio of proof, matched to the routes where their particular wiring will produce the largest positive impact on others. College counseling becomes what it should have been all along: **calling guidance.**

## The Curriculum Claim: Enduring Wisdom, Hyperaccelerated

The differentiation runs through the classroom, too, and it resolves a false choice most schools are stuck in.

On one side, Pacifica teaches the wisdom that has survived the sieve of centuries: Scripture first, and with it the thinkers and works that have shaped how humanity understands truth, beauty, and the good — Plato and Aristotle, Augustine and Aquinas, Dante and Shakespeare, Pascal and Dostoevsky — alongside the critical advances of mathematics and science from Euclid through Newton, Darwin, and Einstein. This canon is not nostalgia; it is quality control. Ideas that have compounded for two thousand years are the safest foundation ever assembled for a sixteen-year-old mind, and they are precisely what AI-first schooling skips.

On the other side, once a student's emerging superpower is identified through their Pathway, we hand them the most powerful tools in human history to develop it — the same frontier AI a professional would use. The aspiring builder ships real software with AI pair-programming; the writer runs her essays through the wringer of a tireless editor; the scientist simulates what previous generations could only imagine; the entrepreneur pressure-tests a real venture. This happens in supervised studio and lab blocks, in person, oriented entirely toward *making* rather than consuming — which is how we hold the line on screens. **Screens for creation, in community, on purpose; never screens for consumption or as a substitute for presence.** The result is students rooted in the oldest wisdom and fluent in the newest tools — formed by the permanent things, accelerated by the latest ones.

## Built by Pacifica: Solid Core, Open Edges

Students are already using AI to build real applications. The next step is infrastructure that turns their ideas into production features of the school day — fast — while teaching them the real AI stack from the inside.

**The hypothesis, stated plainly:** there is no ed-tech product on the market that a well-guided Pacifica student or teacher, working with AI, cannot replace with something better-fit for our school. Every replacement saves subscription money, returns our data to our control, and — most importantly — turns software itself into an act of love of neighbor: students building for the community that is forming them. No other school offers "your ideas ship to production here" as part of the student experience.

**The Forge: from laptop to production in weeks.** GitHub in its raw form is a wall for most students, so we build a school-hosted developer platform with training wheels that teach the real thing: a campus Git server with a friendly interface, project templates, and a one-command pipeline that moves an app from a student's laptop to a shared dev server and — after a review gate — into production for the whole school. The gate is the formation moment: a teacher sponsor, an IT review, and a data-access review before anything ships. Along the way students learn the parts of the AI infrastructure stack almost no high school teaches: version control, environments, deployment, inference servers, APIs, and observability — not from a textbook, but because their classmates are using their app next week.

**Solid core, open edges.** The architecture principle for the whole platform: Pacifica manages the Formation Pathway core — the data model, the privacy architecture, the Formation Framework, the signing flows. But the edges are open. A permissioned API lets student- and teacher-built apps feed the Pathway and draw on scoped, consented data: a chapel reflection kiosk, an intramural scheduler, a peer-tutoring matcher, a service-hours tracker — features we could never dream up on our own, each one widening the lens through which formation is witnessed. Student apps never touch raw Pathway data; they operate in sandboxes with explicit, reviewed data scopes.

**Compute on campus.** The formation data of our students is sacred, and the strongest privacy guarantee is physical: a local cluster (Mac minis today, NVIDIA-class hardware as budgets allow) running the top open-source language models on campus. Inference for student apps costs nothing per query, the data never leaves the building, and students can walk past the machines running their software — the stack made visible, from silicon to shipped feature. Frontier cloud models remain available through enterprise agreements where they are clearly better; sensitive workloads stay home.

**The energy horizon.** The full dream is a campus that generates its own power, its own compute, and its own software. Near-term that means solar and battery offsetting the cluster. On the horizon, as the technology and regulation mature, sits the moonshot: a Radiant-class nuclear microreactor powering the campus — audacious today, and exactly the kind of ambition that tells students, families, and the world what Pacifica believes its people can build. We name it not because it is imminent, but because institutions become what they aim at.

## Where the Platform Sits: The School Technology Stack

A typical private school runs on three tiers of technology, and it clarifies the strategy to name where Formation Pathways sits in relation to each.

**Tier 1 — Systems of record (keep and integrate).** The SIS (PowerSchool/Blackbaud-class: enrollment, transcripts, scheduling), the business office systems (tuition, payroll, accounting), productivity (Google Workspace or Microsoft 365), and safety/compliance systems. These are regulated, boring, and fine. Pathways integrates with them read-only where useful (rosters, schedules) and replaces none of them.

**Tier 2 — Commodity ed-tech (enhance now, replace over time).** This is where schools bleed money on tools that fit no one: the LMS (Canvas), college-counseling platforms (Naviance/SCOIR-class), survey and forms tools, communications apps (mass email, parent messaging), tutoring schedulers, athletics management, chapel/service-hour trackers. This tier is the Forge's hunting ground. Each is a bounded problem a guided student team plus AI can rebuild better-fit for Pacifica — and every replacement converts a recurring subscription into returned budget and a shipped student portfolio. The Pathways Engine replaces the college-counseling platform outright, because calling guidance subsumes college lists.

**Tier 3 — The formation layer (ours, and previously missing).** Above both sits what no school has ever had: Formation Pathways — capture, synthesis, portraits, the Engine, the admissions layer, and the Edges API — running on our own compute, holding the school's most precious data: the witnessed becoming of every student. Tier 1 feeds it rosters; Tier 2 shrinks beneath it as Forge apps take over; Tier 3 is the layer that makes Pacifica *Pacifica*, and the one we will never rent from a vendor.

The strategic summary for the board: we keep the systems of record, we systematically replace the commodity middle with better software our own students build, and we own the formation layer outright — data, models, and mission.

## Presence at Full Strength: Lost Canyon and Pathway Expeditions

If the Formation Pathway is the map, the deepest marks on that map are made when the whole community unplugs. Pacifica already owns the proof.

**Lost Canyon as the keystone.** Every year, every student, teacher, and staff member travels to Lost Canyon, Arizona for four nights and three days of phone-free community — deep conversations about faith, and the seeds of lifelong relationships. Ask any alumnus where their turning point happened, and more often than not the answer is Lost Canyon. Under this proposal, the retreat becomes the formal keystone of every student's Formation Pathway — with a critical rule: **no technology crosses the canyon rim.** Advisors go in carrying two or three questions seeded by each student's Pathway ("this year, watch for how she handles being unhastened; ask him about the calling thread that surfaced in October"). The capture happens afterward, the same way everything in this system does — adults recording what they witnessed, in the days following, feeding the single richest annual harvest of formation moments the Pathway will ever receive. Lost Canyon is where the model shows its character: total presence first, and the technology's only job is to make sure what happened there is never forgotten.

**Pathway Expeditions: the retreat's academic sibling.** The second program is new, and no school in the country offers it. A lead teacher and a second trained adult — a coach, a staff member, or a vetted parent leader — take five or six students, matched by the Pathways Engine for complementary superpowers and formation goals, on a six-to-seven-day expedition in a tech-enabled van: Mammoth for snowboarding, the Sierra for fly fishing, the trails for mountain biking. Two adults on every trip is non-negotiable: it is the supervision standard every serious youth organization holds, it protects students and staff alike, and it means the program is a *model* from day one rather than one teacher's heroic side project. The structure turns travel time and mountain time into the most concentrated learning environment we can build:

The **drives become the classroom.** Five hours each way of Socratic seminar — physics on the way to the mountain, taught by a master teacher to six students with no bells, no periods, and nowhere else to be. Voice AI in the van (tools of the ElevenLabs class) ambiently transcribes the seminar, so the teacher never breaks presence to take a note. That evening, AI turns the transcript into something no conventional classroom can produce: a **unique project for every student**, built from the exact questions *that student* asked, the misconceptions *that student* voiced, and the superpowers their Pathway says are emerging. Students build those projects together at night; days are spent on the mountain or the river learning a life skill school never teaches — and discovering that the physics from the drive is written all over the carve of a snowboard and the arc of a cast.

The pedagogy behind this is not a hunch. Educational research has shown for decades that one-on-one and near-one-on-one tutoring produces dramatically stronger outcomes than classroom instruction — the effect Benjamin Bloom famously measured at two standard deviations. A 1:6 ratio sustained across ten-plus hours of seminar and six days of shared life is tutoring-grade instruction fused with formation-grade relationship. Done right, a single expedition covers a semester course's worth of material *and* delivers what no semester course ever has: a life skill, a band of brothers or sisters, and a teacher who now knows each student well enough to write the richest Pathway entries of their year. For teenagers, it also quietly demolishes the wall between fun and academics — which may be the most valuable lesson of all.

Expeditions slot directly into the Formation Pathways architecture: the Engine composes the rosters, the van's capture feeds the Pathway (under the same consent and human-review rules as everything else), and the projects students build become proof-of-work for the non-traditional routes the Engine surfaces. And note what the van's technology is doing: the students never look at a screen on the drive. They talk, argue, and reason out loud with a teacher — the oldest classroom in human history, the one Socrates ran on foot — while AI listens so that nothing is lost. That is this proposal's principle in its purest form.

## Three Audiences, One Asset

**Current students and families.** Twice a year, alongside the report card, families receive a Pathway Update: a one-page narrative drafted by AI from the semester's observations and edited and signed by the advisor — who your son or daughter is becoming, in the words of eight adults who watched it happen and in your child's own reflections, plus the emerging pathway matches worth exploring and two or three questions to ask at the dinner table. Students engage their Pathway face-to-face: advisors use it to prepare one-on-ones, Lost Canyon leaders personalize challenges from it, expedition teachers build from it, and every senior sits down with their advisor and four years of witnessed growth to author their own transformation story and choose their launch route.

**Prospective families.** Admissions shifts from claiming transformation to displaying it. Beginning Winter 2026–27, the **admissions survey generates a primary Formation Pathway for every candidate** — a starting compass drawn from a family questionnaire and interview notes, so accepted students arrive on day one already seen, and every applying family experiences the platform before they enroll. With consent and careful anonymization, we show the actual arc of a Pacifica education — real portraits, real capstone stories, and a graduate outcomes map that no other school can print: not just college logos, but Thiel-caliber fellowships, funded ventures, apprenticeships, commissions, conservatories, and mission fields, each matched to who that student was made to be. Lost Canyon and Pathway Expeditions become the visible, touchable answer to "what makes Pacifica different?" — no other school on a tour can say *every student goes phone-free in the wilderness with the whole school every year, and a master teacher will take your son to the mountains and teach him a semester of physics on the way.* When a visiting family says "our son is a gifted athlete but spiritually checked out," the team can surface permissioned stories of students who walked in the same way and walked out different.

**The school itself.** Aggregated and anonymized, Pathways data answers questions no Christian school can currently answer: where in the four years growth actually accelerates, which experiences show up most in students' turning points — we predict Lost Canyon and expeditions will dominate that list, and now we can prove it — and where we are strong on truth but thin on grace. This becomes a formation dashboard for leadership and, in time, evidence we can put in front of the broader Christian school movement.

## Proving the Claim

We are asserting something bold: that Formation Pathways are the most productive way to guide a teenager to discover who they truly are and where their largest positive impact lies. Boldness demands evidence, so the platform is built to generate it. We commit to measuring what actually matters: the diversity and fit of graduate pathways (not just college matriculation), the proof-of-work every senior carries out the door, mastery outcomes from expedition courses measured against their classroom equivalents, and — most importantly — longitudinal alumni surveys at one, five, and ten years measuring clarity of purpose, depth of faith, and real-world contribution, benchmarked against national instruments like the Gallup alumni outcome studies. Within five years, Pacifica should be able to say, with data no other school possesses: *our graduates know why they're here, and it shows in what they do for others.*

## Guardrails

**Humans author, AI drafts.** No AI-generated text about a student reaches a family without being reviewed, edited, and signed by a named adult. AI never evaluates a student's soul; it organizes what witnesses saw.

**A compass, never a dossier.** Pathways are held in trust for the student's benefit alone — never used for discipline, ranking, or admissions gatekeeping, never shared without consent, and fully visible to the student, who may respond to anything in it. It is a mirror held up in love.

**Recording with eyes open.** Any ambient capture — including expedition-van seminars — happens only with prior written family consent, is announced and visible to students, is limited to instructional settings (never cabins, meals, or free time), and is processed into instructional material and Pathway entries, with raw audio deleted on a short, published schedule. Sacred spaces stay unrecorded: Lost Canyon remains entirely technology-free, and pastoral conversations are never captured anywhere.

**Privacy by architecture.** FERPA-grade protection, family opt-in with full transparency, enterprise AI tools with contractual guarantees that student data never trains outside models, and legal review of the entire consent structure before launch.

**Formation language is ours.** Faculty and a small theological review group define the framework before any tool touches it, so the categories reflect Pacifica's understanding of grace, truth, and human flourishing — not a vendor's.

**No new student screens.** The platform asks nothing of students digitally. Their encounters with it happen face-to-face; their use of frontier tools happens in supervised, creation-oriented studio blocks; and on drives and mountains, the technology listens so the humans can talk.

## How We Get There

**Fall 2026 — now, this semester.** The **Advanced AI Agents class** is live: students identifying real problems in the school day and shipping software through the full Forge pipeline — local development on their laptops, to the internal dev server, to the external production server. This is not a plan; it is the proof running in a classroom today. In parallel: the Formation Framework drafted by faculty, a lightweight capture tool piloted with eight to ten volunteer teachers on one grade level, this year's Lost Canyon run as the first structured harvest (advisors carry Pathway-seeded questions in, record observations after), and the proof-of-concept Pathway Expedition planned — a lead teacher plus a second adult, six students, one van, one semester course, with pre/post mastery assessment.

**Winter 2026–27 — admissions goes first.** The **admissions survey generates a primary Formation Pathway for every candidate**: a thoughtfully designed family questionnaire plus interview notes, synthesized by AI into a starting compass — who this student is now, what superpowers are emerging, how Pacifica would build on them. Accepted students arrive on day one with a Pathway already begun; admissions conversations shift from transcripts to becoming; and every visiting family experiences the platform before they enroll. This is the single fastest way to make the differentiation tangible to the exact audience that funds the school. The **AI Lab hardware goes live** this quarter (budget below), moving inference on-campus, and the pilot expedition runs over winter break.

**Spring 2027 — pilots complete, foundation built.** All pilot programs conclude with published results: capture (is it under five minutes a week and true?), portraits (do families say "that's my kid"?), the expedition (did the course hold against its classroom twin?), and admissions Pathways (did yield and family feedback move?). The **Formation Pathway Platform foundation is complete**: the data model, consent architecture, signing flows, portrait and Engine generation, and the Edges API — with the first three student-built edge apps from the Agents class shipped to production and the first commercial ed-tech subscription replaced. The board receives the evidence package and the Fall 2027 full-school rollout plan.

**Fall 2027 and beyond — school-wide and outward.** Full-faculty capture, Pathway Updates with every semester report, the Engine live for juniors and seniors, an expedition calendar, admissions rebuilt publicly around evidence of transformation, and the alumni measurement program launched.

## The Endgame: A Platform, Not a Program

Everything above makes Pacifica singular. But the honest ambition is larger, and the Board should see it now, because it changes how we build.

There are thousands of Christian schools in America filled with adults who love God and love teens — schools that have the people but will never have the platform. If the pilot proves what we believe it will, Formation Pathways is not merely Pacifica's edge; it is the missing operating system of Christian formation education. The playbook writes itself: perfect it here for three years, publish the results, then license the platform, the Formation Framework methodology, and the expedition playbook to mission-aligned schools — with Pacifica as the flagship campus, the training ground for the movement, and the standard-bearer.

That endgame has practical consequences today: we build the platform as a product (documented, transferable, school-agnostic in its plumbing even while the theology stays ours), we protect the intellectual property, and we treat every pilot metric as evidence a future partner school would demand. The solid-core/open-edges architecture and the local-first compute stack make the offer to partner schools even stronger: your students' formation data stays on your campus, and your students and teachers extend the platform themselves. Becoming the top Christian high school in the world is the milestone. Equipping a thousand schools like ours to do what we do is the mission — and it is exactly the kind of largest-positive-impact-on-others thinking we are asking of our students.

The public schools will police AI and change nothing. The AI-first schools will accelerate content and hollow out presence. Pacifica alone has the raw material — a building full of adults who love God, love teens, and love the mission, a canyon in Arizona where the whole school unplugs together, and teachers willing to drive six kids to a mountain — to do the thing technology was actually good for all along: making sure that when God transforms a student through the daily faithfulness of this staff, somebody wrote it down, the family got to see it, the student discovered their superpower and the widest possible map of where to deploy it, and the next family deciding on a school saw exactly what is possible.

The transformation is already happening. Formation Pathways makes it visible, provable, and personal — one student at a time.
