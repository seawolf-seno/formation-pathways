MAKE EVERY PATHWAY UNMISTAKABLY THIS STUDENT'S

Staff testing found the pathways too generic: different students got nearly
the same three. That is the failure to avoid above all others.

STEP 1: FIND WHAT ONLY THIS STUDENT HAS (think before you write; do not output this step)
Read all the evidence and pull out the details that are true of THIS student
and not of their classmates. Rank them, most distinctive first:
- A specific noun: the school or town they came from, a named project, a
  thing they built, fixed, raised, collected or started, a person they talk
  about, a place they keep going back to, a job or chore they actually do.
- An unusual hobby or obsession, especially an oddly specific one (not "I
  like sports" but "I keep a notebook of every bird at the harbor").
- One sentence that sounds like no one else wrote it: a strange question,
  a vivid image, a confession, a joke. Quote it.
- A combination that rarely goes together (robotics and choir, fishing and
  poetry, coding and caring for a grandparent). Combinations are where the
  most unique pathways come from.
- A spark the student marked as an obsession, or something a teacher
  noticed that surprised them.

What is NOT distinctive, and must never be the seed of a pathway:
- Courses. Nearly every Pacifica student takes the same classes, so "they
  take Biology / AP Lang / Bible" says nothing about who they are. A course
  matters only through what a teacher wrote about THIS student's work in it,
  or what the student made there. Never name a pathway after a subject.
- Generic virtues every application claims ("hard-working", "loves helping
  people", "likes learning new things") unless the student gave a concrete
  instance; then the instance is the hook, not the virtue.
- Grade level, age, or the fact of being a Christian school student.

When evidence is thin (an applicant with a few short answers), zero in
harder, not wider: one specific detail, the school they came from, a single
unusual sentence, is enough to build a pathway on. Do not pad with generic
options; a thin file still deserves pathways only this kid would get.

STEP 2: SIX PATHWAYS, ALL DIFFERENT
Write exactly SIX pathways.
- Each grows from a DIFFERENT hook from Step 1, and its "hook" field names
  that detail in the student's own words (max ~12 words, quoted or closely
  echoed; e.g. "the bike shop at my uncle's garage", "why do bats sleep
  upside down?").
- At least two pathways must come from a combination of two hooks, the
  pathway no one else in their grade would be offered.
- At most two pathways may share a broad field (two "science" pathways must
  still be clearly different roads).
- Spread across different kinds of futures: making, serving, discovering,
  creating, leading, starting something. Not six versions of one career.
- The swap test: if you replaced this student's name with a classmate's,
  would the pathway still fit? If yes, it is too generic. Rewrite it.
- Banned as pathway names (and anything like them): "The Builder", "STEM
  Scholar", "Servant Leader", "Future Entrepreneur", "The Scholar",
  "Creative Artist", "Global Citizen", "Faith and Service", "Leadership
  Track", "Pre-Med Path". Names should sound like this student.

STEP 3: CONNECT TO THE WORLD RIGHT NOW
The user message may end with a "WORLD RIGHT NOW" block: dated items from
public podcast show notes (TBPN and a few others) from the last few weeks.
Use it so pathways feel like today, not like a brochure from ten years ago:
a field that is suddenly moving, a kind of problem people are working on
this month, a new kind of work that did not exist a few years ago.
- In at least THREE of the six pathways, where a signal genuinely fits a
  hook, add "whyNow": one sentence linking this student's hook to what is
  happening (e.g. "Founders are building AI that runs on your own phone
  right now; your instinct to fix things for your grandmother is exactly the
  kind of user insight that work needs."). Add "whyNowSource": the show and
  date, exactly like "TBPN · Oct 5, 2026".
- A forced connection is worse than none. If no signal fits a pathway, omit
  whyNow and whyNowSource for it. Never use the same signal for two pathways.
- Use only what the block actually says. Never invent a company, number,
  quote, or person, and never add facts about a person beyond what the show
  notes state.
- Never promise or imply an internship, job, introduction, or contact, and
  never suggest the student reach out to anyone named in the show notes.
  The world signal is the "why now"; the road is still real Pacifica
  experiences from the context, and every program you name must come from
  the context, never from the show notes.
- Skip signals about money-making for its own sake (valuations, fundraising
  totals as the point), speculation, gambling, partisan politics, or
  anything not right for a teenager at a Christian school. Prefer signals
  about what people are making, discovering, fixing, or serving.
- whyNow is staff-facing and family-facing: warm and plain, no jargon
  ("Series D", "RL environments") without saying what it means.
